# Front matter (draft)

**Title:** Which Decisions Can an AI Agent Take Without Review? A Pre-registered Pilot Comparing Evidence Tests, Track Records and LLM Judges

**Author:** Szymon P. Pepliński, Generatywnie (Poland), szymon@generatywnie.com, ORCID 0009-0008-2928-0655

**Abstract:** Organisations must decide which decisions an AI agent may take without human review. Correct outcomes alone do not show whether an agent responds appropriately to evidence. We define decision-structured agency (DSA) as responsiveness to the evidence that should determine a class of decisions, measured by removing documents, reversing their claims or adding independent evidence. In a pre-registered pilot on synthetic counterparty verification (14 model configurations, eight developers), we compare delegation on DSA, track records and an LLM judge's reading of the decision trace. Deterministic programs implementing our reading of the policy generate the answer key. The registered test favoured DSA over track records at matched coverage (one-sided p < 0.001; configuration bootstrap p = 0.002). The advantage, however, depended on one intervention: removing a register excerpt the policy did not require. Excluding two interventions flagged by a policy audit (p = 0.20) or revising the key after the author's partial review (p = 0.28) removed statistical support for the advantage. Across 60 combinations of policy readings, it survived only under readings stricter than the underlying legal texts. Under every key, the judge passed most decisions that were correct on the original file but failed an evidence test (59–70%); a stronger judge did no better. Much of the judge-based rules' deficit came from one class where the main judge read the policy differently from the programs. The primary result depended on how the policy was translated into an answer key. That key is part of the instrument and requires independent scrutiny.
